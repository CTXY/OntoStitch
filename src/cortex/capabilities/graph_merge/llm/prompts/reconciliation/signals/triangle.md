# triangle: direct edges and paths

## Trigger, cause, and normal state
A→B→C and A→C coexisting may indicate redundancy or incorrect inference, but topology does not prove transitivity, branch compatibility, or that the direct edge can be deleted.

## General example (illustrative, not this round's graph records or evidence)
A Metric formula references X and Y; even if X depends on Y, M→Y must be retained; works_for and located_in do not entail lives_in.

## Necessary local material
The three nominated directed endpoint-pair assertion sets, their endpoint meanings and branches, and any metadata group referencing those assertions. The path alone does not authorize inspecting unrelated neighbors.

## Writer judgment and modification
Validate inferences against predicate contracts, conditions, and consumers. Fix only evidenced errors; you may add an intermediate business object with a clear source, but do not create nodes just to eliminate a triangle. If a direct assertion is deleted, the final retained graph must independently support the same-scope proposition and source; if it cannot be retained legally, keep.

## Assessment check points
Check the final inference rather than self-justifying via cycles of deleted edges; verify direct input requirements and cross-branch paths; normal triangles do not need to be eliminated.
